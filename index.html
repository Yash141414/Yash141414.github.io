Four rules I keep running into. Click any card to expand.

  

  

    

      01
      
Validate against real costs

      
A strategy that ignores STT, GST and slippage isn't a strategy — it's a chart.

      
Gross edge is the easy part. I test net of the costs that actually hit the ledger, and I would rather kill a candidate at the cost gate than discover it in production. On one research pass, applying real transaction costs filtered out 100% of sub-threshold signals before they ever reached the desk — that filter was the deliverable.

    

    

      02
      
Fail fast, gate early

      
Order the cheap tests before the expensive ones, and let them say no.

      
The VRP framework runs four stages — t-stat, Sharpe, drawdown, cost — each able to reject a hypothesis on its own. The order is deliberate: cheapest and most decisive first. An automated fail-fast cost filter means a dead idea dies in seconds instead of after a week of tuning.

    

    

      03
      
Build for repeatability

      
If the result only exists in your notebook, it isn't a result yet.

      
Reproducible frameworks, documented methodology, reusable templates. The benchmarking harness I built at Nxtgen became the team standard for pre-deployment validation, and 2 reusable inference templates took the repeated setup out of the loop. Auditability isn't bureaucracy — it's what makes a second opinion possible.

    

    

      04
      
Root-cause over patching

      
Fix the generator, not the symptom — then automate the fix away.

      
Recurring timestamp mismatches in minute-level NSE derivatives data could have stayed a standing manual chore. I traced them by hand once, found the mechanism, and wrote the correction script that eliminated the break going forward. The win isn't the clean data — it's never touching it again.
